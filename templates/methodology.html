Model 3.0 · Provisional research rules

Understand the score.

Independent financial-quality and relative-valuation scores, with dated inputs and visible limitations.

Research remains open

All companies with at least one usable score appear in the research list. A low score does not mean a company is unsuitable for every investor. Scores are neither probabilities nor expected returns.

  • Screen match: Quality ≥60 and combined ≥150, with both scores complete.
  • Quality watchlist: Quality ≥70 and outside the strict screen, including companies with incomplete valuation data.
  • Partial data: exactly one complete score. No combined score is invented.
  • Unrated: neither score can be completed, or a separate sector model is required. Reasons stay visible.

These thresholds and weights are provisional. They have not been validated by a historical investment-performance backtest. A new model version is not directly comparable with earlier scores.

Quality · 100 points

FactorWeightRule
Operating return on capital20Latest annual operating income / average of current and previous annual debt + equity. Linear from zero points at 0% to full points at 25%. This is a pre-tax measure, not after-tax ROIC.
Three-year operating margin15Median operating income / revenue across the latest three aligned annual periods; scored relative to peers.
Three-year free-cash-flow margin15Median free cash flow / revenue across those three periods; scored relative to peers.
Cash-flow consistency10Fraction of those three periods with strictly positive free cash flow × 10.
Net debt / EBITDA15(Latest annual debt − cash and short-term investments) / annual EBITDA. Full points at ≤0×; zero at ≥4×; linear between. Known nonpositive EBITDA earns zero.
Annualized revenue growth10Linear from zero at −5% to full points at +20%.
Annualized diluted EPS growth15Linear from zero at 0% to full points at +25%. All three EPS observations must be positive for CAGR; a known nonpositive year earns zero. Missing EPS remains unscored.

Growth = ((latest / earliest)^(1 / elapsed years) − 1) × 100. Three annual reports span approximately two years between endpoints; each adjacent year-end gap must be 300–430 days. EPS reflects diluted shares where the provider supplies them.

Opportunity · 100 points

FactorWeightDefinition
Annual earnings yield35Latest aligned annual net income / current market capitalization × 100.
Annual free-cash-flow yield35Latest aligned annual free cash flow / current market capitalization × 100.
Annual EBITDA / enterprise value30Latest aligned annual EBITDA / simplified enterprise value × 100. Simplified EV = market capitalization + latest annual debt − cash and short-term investments.

Each yield is scored against peers using the rule below. Known negative earnings or cash flow earn zero points for that yield; they are not a missing-data error. Positive denominators and matching financial/quote currencies are required. Simplified EV omits preferred equity and noncontrolling interests and uses annual balance-sheet values, so it can differ from published EV.

Earnings growth appears only in Quality. Analyst targets, trailing/forward P/E, beta and six-month price return are unscored context. Valuation factors are related to each other, and the two overall scores are not statistically independent.

How peer comparisons work

For each peer-scored factor, first look for at least eight other companies in the same industry. If that is unavailable, use at least eight in the same sector. Exclude the company itself. References use the same metric definitions and a positive median; valid negative values remain in the sample.

Points = weight × clamp(company value / peer median / 2, 0, 1). The median earns half the points; twice the median or more earns all points. Nonpositive values earn zero. Linear scoring removes abrupt threshold jumps, but may still compress extreme values at the bounds.

Each report identifies the comparison group, peer tickers, count, median, reference date, and range of fiscal year ends. Annual statements must be no older than 550 days; valuation peers also need market data within seven days. Stored peer references expire after eight days.

The universe is a manually selected 109-company U.S.-listed research sample, not a market index or a representative stock-market census. Industry coverage is uneven; sector fallbacks can compare different business models. Fiscal year ends can differ. Banks, insurers and real-estate businesses need separate models and are not rated. Peer-relative cheapness alone does not establish fair value.

Dates, missing data and evidence

Core inputs come from annual income, cash-flow and balance-sheet statements aligned to identical period-end dates. Free cash flow is the provider line or operating cash flow minus absolute capital expenditure. EBITDA uses the provider line (normalized EBITDA if the standard line is absent), or operating income plus depreciation/amortization when available. These fallbacks can limit accounting comparability.

Quality and Opportunity each require all their own components. Neither substitutes neutral points for unknown values. Quality can remain available when market/valuation data is missing; Opportunity can remain available when a longer quality history is missing. The report shows what is missing for each score.

A full score requires recent aligned annual data. Opportunity also requires a positive price and market capitalization, a price-history date within seven days, and matching financial and quote currencies. Provider data can be revised; retrieval dates are not original publication dates. SEC and company links are verification resources, not a claim that filings have been read automatically.

Weekly publication and validation

The supplied GitHub Actions workflow is scheduled for Sunday at 23:17 UTC. It refreshes the research universe and publishes only when at least 80% of companies return reports and at least 60% have both scores complete. A failed refresh retains the previous snapshot. Zero screen matches is a valid result and never triggers looser thresholds.

The default list places complete score pairs in combined-score order, followed by partial reports. Filters and alternate sorts help explore other candidates. Pagination and report links retain their snapshot identifier. Snapshots older than eight days are visibly marked outdated.

Automated tests check calculation behavior, missing-data handling, persistence and publication safeguards. They do not prove investment performance. Historical validation would need point-in-time fundamentals, a survivorship-aware universe, delisted stocks, transaction costs, an appropriate benchmark and an untouched evaluation period. The package includes a current-snapshot model comparison; this is a sensitivity check, not a backtest.
